Our Compass

Values are not decoration. They are the instrument we navigate by.

Every technology embodies the values of its makers, whether or not they are stated. We would rather state ours — and be held to them. When a decision is hard, this is what we check it against.

Orientation

Right relationship & natural law

Right relationship invites creating technology rooted in mutual respect, ethical reciprocity, and harmony — honoring our interconnectedness with people, communities, and the natural world, and ensuring technology serves our well-being rather than extracting from it.

Natural law invites designing systems that authentically mirror nature's decentralized, self-organizing intelligence — prioritizing harmony over control, emergence over enforcement, and abundance through cooperation. Systems aligned with the world's own regenerative patterns tend to flourish; systems that fight them require ever more force to maintain.

Together they describe the same orientation at two scales: an information ecosystem where decentralization, respect, and adaptability foster resilience — where human technologies serve life rather than exploit it.

These principles are drawn from Indigenous wisdom and teachings — including concepts like Wahkohtowin, Sumak Kawsay, and Ubuntu — that long predate us. We hold them as students, not owners, with gratitude to the peoples and traditions that carry them.

Seven Values

The values that filter every decision

Seven values for seven nodes. They apply to our code, our data model, our licensing, and how we treat each other — the same principles in the social layer and the technical layer, because the two cannot honestly be separated.

  • Free / Libre / Open

    Open source, open standards, open process. Infrastructure this foundational must belong to everyone, or it will end up belonging to someone.

  • Reduce, Reuse, Recycle

    Constrain ambition. Build only what is necessary, reuse what exists, and design so that knowledge and work — yours and others' — can be composted and reused rather than duplicated.

  • Design for Emergence

    A simple set of rules from which rich behavior can grow beats a mountain of hardcoded special cases. We build substrates, not prescriptions, and let structure emerge from use.

  • Transcend and Include

    We don't discard what came before — RDF, hypertext, the open web — we build one level deeper, keeping what works and healing what doesn't. Compatibility is a form of respect.

  • Care for and Share the Sources

    Provenance, attribution, and consent are first-class. Knowledge has lineage; honoring where things come from is built into the data model, not bolted on.

  • Meeting People Where They Are

    Radical compatibility with existing tools, files, and habits. No rip-and-replace, no demand that you adopt our worldview to benefit. The tool adapts to the person, not the reverse.

  • Mirror Nature

    When we face a design question, we ask how the analogous living system works — decentralized, adaptive, cooperative — and take that as our starting hypothesis. Biomimicry, not metaphor.

This compass is a living document. We are still refining it — including the relationship between these values and the concrete affordances of the software — and we expect it to be sharpened by the people who join us.

Hold us to these.

The most useful thing a community can do with a compass is check the navigators against it. If you see us drifting, say so.
